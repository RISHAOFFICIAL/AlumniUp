# AlumniUp: Personas (Stage 1)

Status: APPROVED by Risha, October 19, 2026.
Process: Risha's App Building Playbook, Stage 1.
One primary persona (the donor), three secondary personas who use
the app in clearly different ways (poster, approver, sponsor).

## Primary persona: the alumni donor

**Denise Carter, 45, King High Class of 1998.**
Lives in Atlanta now (the Detroit diaspora). Registered nurse and
program coordinator. Married, two kids, gives to her church and a
couple of causes a year. Heard about AlumniUp from a classmate's
Facebook post.

- Goals: give back to the specific school that made her, in amounts
  she controls ($25 to $100 at a time). Feel connected to Detroit
  again. See her class year represented.
- Frustrations: she has donated to school fundraisers before and
  never learned what happened to the money. General "support our
  schools" asks feel like a black hole. She is tired of GoFundMe
  links she cannot verify.
- Tech comfort: high on her phone, low patience. iPhone, Safari and
  Facebook are her internet. She will not download an app for a
  one-time donation, but she will bookmark a site that earns it.
- What success looks like: she funds part of a named need at King
  High, gets her tax receipt by email instantly, watches the total
  climb, and one day sees the need marked Received by the school.
  Her name and '98 appear on the Wall of Honor and the Class of
  1998 passes 1994 on the leaderboard.
- She will never: give without being able to track whether the
  school received the money; tolerate daily begging emails; create
  an account before she can even see the needs; pay through anything
  that does not give her a tax receipt.
- Accessibility: reads on her phone in bright light; needs strong
  contrast and text that survives larger system font sizes.

## Secondary persona: the coach who posts needs

**Coach Darnell Williams, 38, varsity coach, Detroit public high
school.** Fifteen years in the district. Buys balls, tape, and bus
snacks from his own paycheck most seasons.

- Goals: get his field repaired and his equipment funded without
  spending his own money or running a car wash every month.
- Frustrations: district paperwork, fundraisers that take weeks of
  his evenings, and asking parents who have less than he does.
- Tech comfort: medium. Android phone, school Chromebook, email when
  he has to. If a form takes more than about 15 minutes or needs a
  training video, he will abandon it and go back to paying himself.
- What success looks like: posts a need with a phone photo in one
  sitting, gets verified once, watches donations come in, and
  confirms receipt with one tap when the money lands.
- He will never: front the money again once this works; post
  anything that makes his school look begging rather than proud;
  use a tool his principal has not blessed.
- Accessibility: school Wi-Fi is slow; pages must load on weak
  connections. He often completes tasks one handed on a field.

## Secondary persona: the administrator who stands behind it

**Dr. Angela Foster, 52, principal, Detroit public high school.**
Accountable to the district for anything carrying her school's name.

- Goals: attract alumni support to her school while keeping every
  public claim about her school accurate and approved. Confirm
  receipt of funds cleanly for her records.
- Frustrations: third parties fundraising "for" her school without
  permission; no visibility into what was raised in her school's
  name; audit questions she cannot answer.
- Tech comfort: medium-high, desktop first (office PC, district
  email), phone second.
- What success looks like: she reviews and approves her staff's
  needs before they go public, sees totals raised in her school's
  name at any time, and confirms receipt in the same system the
  public watches, so her confirmation is the public record.
- She will never: allow unverified posts under her school's name;
  let money move without a paper trail she can show the district;
  endorse a platform that hides fees or totals from her.

## Secondary persona: the corporate sponsor

**James Okafor, 41, community relations manager at a Detroit-area
company.** Owns a local giving budget and reports its impact to
leadership quarterly.

- Goals: put his company's name behind Detroit schools in a way that
  is brand safe, tax clean, and easy to report: dollars in, schools
  reached, needs completed.
- Frustrations: sponsorship offers that are really ad buys; no proof
  of delivery; having to chase nonprofits for receipts and summaries.
- Tech comfort: high, desktop and email; his assistant does the
  paperwork, so everything must be forwardable and printable.
- What success looks like: he picks a tier, his logo appears where
  promised, and at quarter end he downloads a one-page impact
  summary showing totals given and needs marked Received.
- He will never: sponsor a platform whose money trail he cannot
  show his CFO; accept recognition without verification behind it.

## What the personas agree on (design law for later stages)

1. Nobody trusts what they cannot track. The public money trail
   (total given, funded, school-confirmed Received) is the product.
2. Nobody has time. Posting a need, donating, and confirming
   receipt are each a few minutes, one sitting, no training.
3. Pride, not pity. Schools and alumni are presented as strong
   communities investing in their own, never as charity cases.
4. The phone is the storefront. Every persona except the
   administrator is phone first, often on weak connections.
